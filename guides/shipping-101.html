Shipping and Packing 101 for Marketplace Sellers

Ship marketplace orders safely and affordably: weigh and measure, understand dimensional weight, pack to survive transit, buy labels, track and insure.

By the Market.Place editors·6 min read·Updated 2026-10-31

Shipping is where many new sellers lose money, time and reviews. A parcel that is weighed wrongly costs extra postage; one that is packed badly arrives broken and turns into a refund. The fix is a simple, repeatable process that you follow for every order.

Key takeaways

  • Weigh and measure the packed parcel, not the item alone, before buying a label.
  • Large, light boxes can be billed by dimensional weight, so use the smallest box that protects the item.
  • Pack so nothing moves: padding on all sides, a sturdy outer box and taped seams.
  • Buy labels with tracking, ideally through the marketplace, and ship within your handling time.
  • Photograph high-value items and packing before sealing; it helps if a dispute arises.

Set up a simple shipping station

You do not need a warehouse. A corner of a table with the right supplies saves a lot of hunting around.

Essentials:

  • A digital scale that reads small increments.
  • A tape measure.
  • A tape gun and good quality packing tape.
  • A selection of boxes and padded or poly mailers in a few common sizes.
  • Padding: bubble wrap, kraft paper, air pillows or clean recycled materials.
  • Scissors or a box cutter.
  • A marker for labelling inner bags or noting order numbers.

Optional upgrades: a thermal label printer, adhesive label sheets, a small shelf for supplies and a basket for parcels waiting to go out.

Weighing and measuring correctly

Postage is based on the parcel as it will travel, so measure after packing.

  1. Pack the item fully, including padding and any inserts.
  2. Weigh the sealed parcel. Round up to the next increment the carrier or platform uses.
  3. Measure length, width and height of the outside of the box at the longest points.
  4. Enter these into the platform's shipping tool or the carrier's.

If you charge buyers for shipping at the time of listing, estimate the packed weight and size in advance. Underestimating means you pay the difference; overestimating can put buyers off.

Understanding dimensional weight

Many carriers do not just charge by how heavy a parcel is. They also consider how much space it takes up. This is called dimensional weight (or volumetric weight).

The concept is straightforward. The carrier multiplies the parcel's length, width and height, then divides by a set number to get a weight equivalent. They then bill you for whichever is greater: the actual weight or the dimensional weight. Each carrier sets its own formula and rules, and those can change, so check the carrier's current guidance.

What this means for you:

  • A big box with a light item inside may cost much more than you expect.
  • Right-sizing the box is the easiest way to save money. Leave enough room for padding, not empty air.
  • Soft items such as clothing often ship best in mailers rather than boxes.

Packing to survive the journey

Assume your parcel will be dropped, stacked under heavier boxes and handled by machines. Pack for that.

The core rules

  1. Protect the item first. Wrap fragile items individually. Tape loose parts or put them in a small bag so nothing rattles.
  2. Waterproof when needed. Place clothing, paper goods and electronics in a plastic bag before boxing.
  3. Use a sturdy outer box. Thin retail boxes and damaged cartons are not designed for shipping.
  4. Fill the gaps. Add padding until the item cannot shift when you shake the box gently.
  5. Keep padding on all six sides. Leave space between the item and every wall of the box.
  6. Tape properly. Seal all seams on the top and bottom, not just the centre line. Use packing tape, not household tape.

Fragile items: the double-box method

For glass, ceramics and delicate electronics, wrap the item and place it in a snug inner box. Then put that box inside a larger outer box with padding on every side. The gap between boxes absorbs shocks that would otherwise reach the item.

A little presentation goes a long way

You do not need branded tissue paper, but a tidy parcel helps. Fold clothing neatly, remove price tags from other shops, and add a short thank-you note if you like. Buyers often mention a well-packed parcel in reviews.

Labels, tracking and handling time

Buying labels through the marketplace is usually the smoothest option. Tracking uploads to the order automatically, the buyer gets notified and you have a clear record if something goes wrong.

Whichever route you take:

  • Always use tracked shipping where available, especially for anything you could not afford to refund.
  • Print clearly and attach the label flat on the largest surface, not across seams.
  • Remove or cover old labels and barcodes on reused boxes.
  • Ship within your handling time. Late shipping hurts your account standing on many platforms and is a common source of complaints.
  • Ship only to the address on the order. If a buyer asks you to send it somewhere else, read why that can be a scam in how sellers avoid scams.

Drop off parcels at the carrier or arrange a pickup, and keep any acceptance receipt until the buyer confirms delivery.

Insurance, coverage and claims

Some labels include basic coverage for loss or damage, while extra coverage can usually be purchased. Before relying on any of it, read:

  • What the coverage limit is and what it excludes.
  • What proof you will need, such as receipts, photos of packing or the damaged item.
  • How long you have to file a claim.

For high-value items, consider requiring a signature on delivery if your platform and carrier support it, and check your platform's rules on when signature confirmation is expected for seller protection.

Photographing the item and the packed parcel before sealing takes seconds and gives you evidence if a buyer claims damage or that the box arrived empty.

When a parcel goes missing or arrives damaged

Even good packing occasionally meets bad luck. Stay calm and work through the platform's process.

  1. Check tracking first. Parcels sometimes show delays or are delivered to a neighbour or a pickup point.
  2. Reply to the buyer promptly inside the marketplace's messages, and tell them what you are doing.
  3. Ask for photos of any damage, including the outer box and the packing materials.
  4. Follow the platform's resolution steps before taking any action off-platform. Many marketplaces have specific timelines and require you to respond within them.
  5. File a carrier claim if coverage applies, using your packing photos and receipts as evidence.

Keep the outcome in your records. If one item type keeps arriving damaged, change how you pack it.

Shipping internationally

Selling abroad widens your market but adds steps:

  • Customs forms describing contents and value accurately.
  • Possible duties and taxes for the buyer, which should be explained in your listing.
  • Longer transit times and more complicated returns.
  • Restrictions on certain items, such as batteries, liquids or some materials.

Many marketplaces offer international shipping programs that handle customs paperwork for you. If not, use the carrier's official tools and never under-declare value to save the buyer money.

Your per-order shipping checklist

  1. Confirm payment in the marketplace, not via email.
  2. Check the order address.
  3. Inspect and photograph the item.
  4. Wrap, waterproof and pad it in a right-sized box or mailer.
  5. Shake test: nothing should move.
  6. Seal all seams.
  7. Weigh and measure the packed parcel.
  8. Buy a tracked label and attach it flat.
  9. Mark the order as shipped and add tracking if needed.
  10. Drop off within your handling time and keep the receipt.

Getting shipping right supports everything else: better reviews, fewer refunds and more confidence in your pricing. For costs, factor postage into your price with the fee calculator, and if you are scaling up, see starting a reselling business for how to organise supplies and inventory.

Frequently asked questions

What is dimensional weight in simple terms?

It is a billing weight calculated from a parcel's size rather than how heavy it is. Carriers use it so large, light boxes are priced fairly for the space they take up, and they typically charge whichever is greater, the actual or the dimensional weight.

Should I buy shipping labels through the marketplace or directly from the carrier?

Buying through the marketplace is usually simplest, because tracking uploads automatically and it may help in seller protection claims. Some sellers buy elsewhere for specific services, but then you must add tracking to the order yourself.

Is it worth adding insurance or extra coverage?

For higher-value or fragile items it often is, but read what the coverage actually includes, how claims work and what proof is required. Some labels include limited coverage by default, and good packing is still your best protection.

Can I reuse old boxes for shipping?

Yes, if they are sturdy, dry and undamaged. Remove or fully cover old labels and barcodes so the parcel is not misrouted, and do not reuse boxes that are crushed or have lost their rigidity.
