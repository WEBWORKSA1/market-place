How Buyers Avoid Marketplace Scams: A Red-Flag Guide

Spot and avoid common marketplace scams as a buyer: fake listings, off-platform payment, gift cards, fake escrow, deposit tricks and counterfeits.

By the Market.Place editors·6 min read·Updated 2026-10-31

Most marketplace transactions go smoothly, but scammers rely on buyers being excited, rushed or trusting. The tricks repeat themselves, and once you know the patterns they become easy to spot. This guide walks through the common scams, the warning signs and what to do if something goes wrong.

Key takeaways

  • Keep messages and payment inside the marketplace; most protection ends the moment you leave.
  • Never pay with gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency or "friends and family" payments for a purchase from a stranger.
  • A price far below the market is a reason for extra caution, not excitement.
  • Fake escrow sites and fake delivery agents are designed to look official; go to services directly, never through a seller's link.
  • If you are scammed, act fast: contact your bank or payment provider, report the account and keep all evidence.

Why staying on the platform matters

Marketplaces that handle payment usually offer some form of buyer protection, such as refunds if an item never arrives or is significantly not as described. Those programs typically apply only when you pay through the platform's own checkout and keep the conversation in its messaging system.

That is exactly why scammers try to move you elsewhere. A seller who says "text me instead" or "it is cheaper if you pay me directly" is often trying to remove the safety net. Even if the request seems harmless, the answer should be no.

The most common buyer scams

Fake listings

A fake listing advertises an item that the seller does not have. Photos are often copied from other listings or retail sites, and the price is usually attractive enough to create urgency. The seller collects payment, then disappears.

Watch for: brand-new or recently created accounts, no reviews, stock photos for used items, refusal to provide extra photos, and pressure to pay before others do.

Off-platform payment requests

The seller asks you to pay by bank transfer, a payment app, or a link they send. Sometimes they claim the platform's checkout is "broken" or that fees are too high.

Watch for: any reason to avoid the official checkout. For shipping marketplaces, there is no good reason a legitimate seller needs this.

Gift card payments

The seller asks you to buy gift cards and send them the codes. Gift cards are popular with fraudsters because they are fast to spend and very hard to trace.

Watch for: any request involving gift card codes. This alone is enough to walk away.

Fake escrow services

For expensive items such as vehicles, machinery or electronics, a scammer may suggest using an "escrow" service to hold your money until you receive the item. They then send a link to a convincing fake website they control.

Watch for: a seller who insists on a specific escrow site, sends you a link, or rejects escrow providers you choose. Legitimate escrow services can be found by searching independently, and you should never follow a link from the seller.

Deposit and shipping-agent scams

The seller says the item is in demand and asks for a deposit to hold it, or claims it must be shipped through a "delivery agent" that needs an upfront fee. Once paid, more fees appear, then the seller vanishes.

Watch for: urgency, multiple fees, and a third party you have never heard of.

Overpayment and refund tricks

A newer twist targets buyers. The scammer claims they accidentally overcharged you or sent too large a refund, then asks you to send back the difference. The original payment is later reversed or turns out never to have happened.

Watch for: any request to return money. Check your actual account balance through your bank or payment app, not through screenshots or emails.

Counterfeit goods

Fakes are common in designer fashion, sneakers, cosmetics, electronics accessories and collectibles. Some sellers knowingly list fakes as genuine; others do not realise.

Watch for: prices well below the authentic market, blurry or missing photos of labels and serial numbers, vague answers about origin, and many identical "new" items from one seller. Use platforms with authentication programs for high-value branded goods where available.

Red-flag checklist

Stop and reconsider if you notice any of these:

  1. The seller asks to move the conversation to text, email or another app.
  2. They want payment by gift card, wire transfer, cryptocurrency or a "friends and family" payment.
  3. The price is far below what similar items sold for.
  4. The account is brand new, has no reviews, or has reviews that look copied.
  5. Photos look like stock images or appear elsewhere online.
  6. The seller refuses a new photo or a video call showing the item.
  7. There is heavy urgency: "lots of interest", "today only", "send a deposit now".
  8. They send you a link to pay, to an escrow service or to a shipping agent.
  9. The story keeps changing, such as a seller who is suddenly overseas or in the military.
  10. They ask you to return an "overpayment".

Due diligence before you buy

A few minutes of checking can save you from most problems.

  • Check sold prices. Compare the asking price with what similar items have actually sold for. The pricing strategy guide explains how sellers find comps; buyers can use the same method.
  • Review the seller's profile. Look at account age, number of reviews, and what else they have listed.
  • Reverse image search the main photo to see if it appears elsewhere.
  • Ask specific questions only a real owner could answer, such as the serial number location, battery health or a measurement.
  • Request a custom photo showing the item next to a note with today's date.
  • Read the listing carefully for phrases like "for parts", "as is" or "box only".

For local deals, inspect the item in person before paying and follow our safe local meetups guide.

Extra checks for expensive purchases

The higher the price, the more time it is worth spending. For phones, laptops, cameras, bikes, designer items or vehicles:

  • Ask for the serial number or IMEI and, where your country offers one, check it against a stolen-device or lost-property register before you pay.
  • Confirm that phones, tablets and laptops are signed out of the previous owner's accounts and not locked, since a locked device may be unusable.
  • Ask for proof of purchase, such as an original receipt, for luxury goods.
  • Prefer platforms that offer authentication for the category, or have the item checked by a reputable specialist.
  • For vehicles, follow your local rules for history checks and ownership transfer.

If something goes wrong

Speed matters. Do these as soon as you suspect a scam:

  1. Contact your bank, card issuer or payment provider and explain what happened. Ask whether the payment can be stopped or disputed.
  2. Open a case with the marketplace if you paid through it, and report the seller's account either way.
  3. Save all evidence: screenshots of the listing, messages, payment receipts and any links you were sent.
  4. Change passwords if you entered login details on a suspicious site, and turn on two-factor authentication.
  5. Report the fraud to your national service. In the US, the Federal Trade Commission explains next steps in What To Do if You Were Scammed. In Canada, you can report fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. Elsewhere, use your national consumer protection or police fraud service.

Be wary of anyone who contacts you afterwards offering to "recover" your money for a fee. That is often a second scam aimed at the same victims.

Buying confidently

Scams depend on pressure and secrecy. Slow down, keep everything on the platform and verify before you pay. Sellers face their own set of tricks, covered in how sellers avoid scams and fraud, and understanding both sides makes you a sharper buyer. Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to pay a seller directly with a payment app?

It can be risky for strangers, because some payment apps are designed for friends and family and offer little or no protection for purchases. On shipping marketplaces, pay through the platform's own checkout so its buyer protection applies.

What should I do if I think I have been scammed?

Contact your bank or payment provider immediately, report the account to the marketplace, and save every message and receipt. Then report it to your national fraud reporting service, such as the FTC in the US or the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

How can I tell if a listing photo is stolen?

Run a reverse image search on the photo and look for the same image on other listings or retail sites. Asking the seller for a new photo with a handwritten note showing their username and today's date is another quick test.

Why do scammers ask for gift cards?

Gift card codes can be spent or resold quickly and are very hard to trace or reverse. No legitimate marketplace seller should require payment by gift card.
