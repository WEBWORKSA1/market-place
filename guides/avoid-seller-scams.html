How Sellers Avoid Scams, Chargebacks and Marketplace Fraud

Protect your sales from fake payment emails, overpayment tricks, chargebacks, address changes, return fraud and account takeover with this seller playbook.

By the Market.Place editors·6 min read·Updated 2026-10-31

Scams aimed at sellers are designed to get you to ship an item before you are really paid, to send money back, or to give up control of your account. They succeed because they look routine. Once you know the patterns and set a few firm rules, most of them stop working on you.

Key takeaways

  • Confirm payment by logging in to the platform yourself, never from an email, text or screenshot.
  • Never refund an "overpayment" or send money to a buyer for any reason outside the platform.
  • Ship only to the order address, with tracking, and keep evidence of condition and packing.
  • Stay inside the marketplace's messaging and checkout; it is where your seller protection lives.
  • Secure your account with a unique password and two-factor authentication.

The rule behind every rule

Almost every seller scam needs you to do one of three things: ship without confirmed payment, move off the platform, or send money or account access. If a buyer's request leads to any of those, stop and check. Legitimate buyers rarely need any of them.

Payment scams: fake emails, overpayments and chargebacks

These three scams all target the moment money changes hands. Each one tries to convince you that you have been paid when you have not, or that you owe money back.

Fake payment emails and screenshots

A buyer says they have paid and you receive an email that looks like it is from the marketplace or a payment service. It might say funds are "pending" until you provide a tracking number, or that you need to click a link to "accept" the payment.

These emails are fake. They are designed to get you to ship, or to steal your login details through a lookalike website.

How to protect yourself:

  • Open the marketplace or payment app yourself and check the order or balance there.
  • Do not click payment links in emails or messages.
  • Ignore screenshots of "completed" payments; they are trivial to fake.
  • Be suspicious of any message saying funds will release only after you ship.

Overpayment scams

The buyer sends, or claims to send, more than the asking price, then asks you to refund the difference or to pay their "shipping agent" from the extra. The original payment is fake, stolen or later reversed, and you lose both the money you sent and possibly the item.

How to protect yourself: never accept more than the agreed amount, never send money to a buyer or a third party, and treat any story involving an overpayment as a scam. This is especially common in local classifieds where sellers deal directly with buyers.

Chargebacks and payment reversals

A chargeback happens when a buyer asks their card issuer or bank to reverse a payment. Sometimes it is a genuine problem. Sometimes it is "friendly fraud": the buyer received the item but claims they did not, or claims they did not authorise the purchase.

Some payment methods, especially bank transfers on certain apps, can also be reversed if the account used was compromised.

How to protect yourself:

  • Sell through the platform's checkout, which often comes with seller protection when you meet its requirements.
  • Ship with tracking to the order address, and use signature confirmation for high-value items where it is available and appropriate.
  • Keep photos of the item and packing, plus all messages, in case you need to respond.
  • Respond promptly and thoroughly to any dispute notice.

Each platform publishes its own seller protection requirements, so read them for your marketplace. Our marketplace profiles link to the relevant policies.

Shipping and return scams

The second family of scams happens after payment, around where the item goes and what comes back.

Address change requests

After paying, a buyer messages asking you to ship to a different address, often a relative, a workplace or a freight forwarder. Sometimes the buyer's account was used fraudulently and the scammer wants the item sent to themselves. Other times the goal is to make a "not received" claim, since the item went somewhere other than the order address.

How to protect yourself: ship only to the address shown on the order. If the buyer needs a change, ask them to update it through the platform, or cancel and let them reorder. Polite but firm wording works: "For security I can only ship to the address on the order. If you need a different address, you can cancel and reorder."

Return fraud

Return fraud takes several forms:

  • Switch fraud: the buyer returns a different, older or broken item instead of the one you sent.
  • Empty box: the buyer claims the parcel was empty, or returns an empty box.
  • "Not as described" abuse: the buyer claims damage or a defect that was not there, to get a refund while keeping the item or after using it.
  • Wardrobing: an item is used once, for an event, then returned.

How to protect yourself:

  1. Photograph each item from all angles before shipping, including serial numbers.
  2. Record unique identifiers such as serial numbers, IMEI numbers, or small marks in your records.
  3. Photograph or film the packing process for high-value items.
  4. Describe condition precisely in the listing; see writing listings that sell.
  5. Inspect returns on arrival and photograph the parcel and contents before opening fully.
  6. Report suspected fraud to the platform with your evidence.

Account takeover and phishing

Your selling account and the email address behind it are valuable. If a scammer gets in, they can change payout details, list fake items under your good reputation or message your buyers.

Phishing usually arrives as a message that looks official: a "policy violation", a "suspended account", a "verification required" notice or a buyer sending a link to "see the problem". Some scammers even ask for a verification code sent to your phone, claiming they need it to confirm you are real.

How to protect yourself:

  • Use a unique, strong password for your selling account and for the linked email.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere it is offered.
  • Never share a verification code with anyone, including someone claiming to be support.
  • Go to the platform directly rather than clicking links in messages.
  • Check your account's payout details and active sessions regularly.

Seller red-flag checklist

Pause before shipping if you see any of these:

Red flag What it usually means Safe response
"Check your email for my payment" Fake payment notification Confirm payment in the app or platform yourself
Buyer sends extra and asks for change Overpayment scam Decline; never send money back
Request to ship to a new address Fraud or setup for a claim Ship only to the order address
"Text me" or "pay you on another app" Removing platform protection Keep everything on the platform
Buyer asks for a code sent to your phone Account or identity takeover Never share codes
Urgency, sob stories, or a third-party courier Pressure tactic Slow down and verify
New account buying a high-value item with odd requests Higher fraud risk Follow every rule strictly; ask the platform if unsure

If you have been targeted

  1. Do not ship if payment is not confirmed on the platform.
  2. Report the buyer through the marketplace's reporting tools.
  3. Secure your accounts if you clicked a link or shared details: change passwords and review payout and contact information.
  4. Contact your bank if money left your account.
  5. Save evidence: messages, emails, order details and screenshots.
  6. Report the fraud to your national service, such as the US Federal Trade Commission, which explains steps in What To Do if You Were Scammed, or the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.

Building scam-resistant habits

The best defence is a routine you follow for every order, regardless of how friendly the buyer seems. Confirm payment, ship to the order address with tracking, keep everything on the platform and document condition. Our shipping guide folds these steps into a per-order checklist, and if you sell locally, the safe local meetups guide covers in-person risks. Frequently asked questions

How do I know a buyer has really paid?

Log in to the marketplace or payment app directly, without using a link from an email or message, and check that the order shows as paid. Never ship based on an email, text or screenshot alone.

A buyer asked me to ship to a different address. Should I?

Generally no. Ship only to the address on the order, because seller protection on many platforms depends on it; if the buyer genuinely needs a change, they can cancel and reorder with the correct address.

What is a chargeback?

A chargeback is when a buyer asks their card issuer or bank to reverse a payment. Legitimate chargebacks protect consumers, but some buyers file false ones after receiving an item, so tracking, photos and keeping communication on the platform are important evidence.

What should I do if my selling account is hacked?

Reset your password immediately from the official site or app, review your payout details, email address and active sessions, and contact the platform's support. Turn on two-factor authentication and change passwords on any account that shared the same password.
