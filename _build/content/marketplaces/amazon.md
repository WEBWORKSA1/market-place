## At a glance

- A catalog-driven retail site where many sellers often compete on the same product page.
- The Professional plan costs $39.99 a month; the Individual plan swaps that for $0.99 per item sold.
- Referral fees run 15% in most categories, with lower or tiered rates in a handful of others.
- Optional Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) handles storage, packing and shipping for an extra fee.
- Strict performance metrics: late shipments, cancellations and complaints can put an account at risk.

## Who it's best for

Amazon suits sellers who treat selling as a business rather than a weekend clear-out. If you have repeatable inventory, such as your own branded goods, wholesale stock or retail arbitrage finds that you can buy again, the volume of shoppers can justify the higher costs. It is also a strong fit for anyone willing to use FBA, because Prime-eligible listings tend to be what shoppers filter for.

It is a weaker choice for one-off used items, handmade pieces that need a story, or sellers who want a casual, low-commitment experience. Account setup involves identity and tax verification, and some categories and brands are gated until you get approval.

## What it costs to sell

Amazon charges two main kinds of fees. The first is the selling plan. On the Professional plan you pay $39.99 every month whether or not you sell anything. On the Individual plan there is no monthly charge, but you pay $0.99 for each item sold. If you expect to sell more than about 40 items a month, the Professional plan usually works out cheaper and unlocks more tools.

The second is the referral fee, a percentage of the total price the buyer pays, including any shipping or gift-wrap charges. For most categories, including home and kitchen, toys, sports and tools, the rate is 15%, with a $0.30 minimum per item. Some categories differ: computers and consumer electronics are 8%, clothing is tiered by price (5%, 10% or 17%), and beauty and personal care items are 8% at $10 or less and 15% above. Media items such as books also carry a $1.80 closing fee per item.

**Worked example ($50 item, no separate shipping charge, Professional plan, general category):**

- Referral fee: 15% × $50 = $7.50
- You receive: $50.00 − $7.50 = **$42.50** before the monthly plan fee, fulfillment costs and advertising.

If you sell 40 items a month, the $39.99 subscription adds roughly $1.00 per item. FBA fulfillment, storage and advertising are separate and can add significantly to the total.

Fees verified October 2026 on Amazon's official [seller pricing page](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing).

## Strengths

- **Unmatched traffic.** Shoppers often start their product searches here, so demand already exists for most mainstream products.
- **Fulfillment options.** FBA lets small sellers offer fast, Prime-eligible shipping without running a warehouse.
- **Trust.** Buyers who would hesitate to buy from an unknown website will often buy from an unknown seller on Amazon.
- **Scalable tools.** Professional sellers get bulk listing, reports, advertising and API access.

## Watch-outs

- **Fees stack up.** The 15% referral fee is just the start; fulfillment, storage, returns processing and ads can push total costs well above that.
- **Price competition.** When several sellers share one listing, the Buy Box tends to go to whoever offers the best combination of price, delivery speed and performance.
- **Account health.** Metrics are enforced, and suspensions can freeze payouts while you appeal.
- **Category gating.** Some categories and brands require approval, invoices or certifications before you can list.
- **Returns.** Buyers expect easy returns, and you will often absorb the cost.

## Tips to sell more here

1. Calculate your break-even including referral, fulfillment and an ad budget before you buy stock; a product that only works at 15% margin rarely survives.
2. Choose the right plan: stay on Individual until you reliably sell about 40 units a month, then switch to Professional.
3. Write titles that lead with brand, product type and the key spec shoppers search for, and fill every relevant attribute field so filters pick you up.
4. Use at least six clear images, including the main image on a pure white background and others that show scale and use.
5. Consider FBA for small, fast-moving items where Prime eligibility makes a real difference; keep bulky or slow items in your own fulfillment.
6. Ship on time and respond to buyer messages within 24 hours; strong metrics help you win the Buy Box.
7. Register your brand if you own one, which unlocks enhanced content and some protection against listing hijacking.

## Buyer protection in brief

Amazon's A-to-z Guarantee covers buyers when an order from a third-party seller does not arrive or is materially different from the listing. Buyers usually contact the seller first, then file a claim if the problem is not resolved. Amazon decides the outcome and can debit the seller's account. For sellers, this means clear, accurate listings and tracked shipping are your best defense. FBA orders are handled by Amazon's own customer service, which can shift some of the dispute handling off your plate, though refund costs can still land on you.
