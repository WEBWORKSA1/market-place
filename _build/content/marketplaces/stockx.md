## At a glance

- Sellers ship items to StockX, where they are checked before going to the buyer.
- Prices are set by bids and asks, like a stock exchange, rather than individual listings.
- Transaction fee starts at 9% (Level 1) and falls to 7% at the highest seller level.
- A 3% payment processing fee applies to every sale, with a $5 US minimum seller fee.
- Focused on new, deadstock sneakers, streetwear, trading cards, collectibles and some electronics.

## Who it's best for

StockX is aimed at resellers of new, in-demand products, especially limited sneakers and streetwear. Because the price for each size and item is set by the market, you do not need to write descriptions or take photos; you just match a product, choose a size and set your ask. Sellers moving volume can work their way up the seller levels to reduce fees.

It is not a fit for used, worn or one-off items without a catalog entry, and sellers who want direct contact with buyers or flexible pricing will find it restrictive.

The exchange model also suits people who value speed over maximum price. Selling into an existing bid is close to instant, which can be useful when you need to turn inventory into cash quickly or want to lock in a price before the market for a release cools off.

## What it costs to sell

StockX charges two fees on its verified channel. The first is a transaction fee based on your seller level, calculated on the final sale price. Level 1 sellers pay 9%. The rate falls to 8.5% at Level 2 (12 sales and $1,500 in a quarter), 8% at Level 3, 7.5% at Level 4 and 7% at Level 5, which requires 800 sales and $100,000 in quarterly volume.

The second is a 3% payment processing fee. StockX also states a $5.00 minimum seller fee in the US; on our reading it applies to the transaction fee, which matters for items under about $55. StockX also runs newer Listings and Live channels that, at launch, charged select sellers no standard fees; those are not covered here.

**Worked example ($50 item, Level 1):**

- Transaction fee: 9% × $50 = $4.50, which is below the $5.00 minimum, so $5.00 applies
- Payment processing: 3% × $50 = $1.50
- Total fees: $5.00 + $1.50 = $6.50
- You receive: $50.00 − $6.50 = **$43.50**.

On a $200 pair at Level 1, the math is 9% ($18.00) + 3% ($6.00) = $24.00 in fees.

Fees verified October 2026 on StockX's official [seller fees help article](https://stockx.com/help/articles/what-are-stockxs-fees-for-sellers).

## Strengths

- **Verification builds trust.** Buyers pay more confidently when items are checked first.
- **No listing work.** No photos or descriptions needed for catalog items.
- **Transparent market prices.** Bid and sale history help you price quickly.
- **Volume discounts.** Seller levels reward consistent sellers with lower fees.
- **Global demand.** Buyers in many countries trade on the platform.

## Watch-outs

- **Penalties.** Failing verification, shipping late or cancelling sales can lead to fees and lower standing.
- **Minimum fee bites on cheap items.** The $5 floor makes low-priced items expensive to sell.
- **Price competition.** Asks are visible, so margins can shrink quickly on popular items.
- **Payout timing.** You are paid after the item passes verification.
- **Condition limits.** Most categories accept only new, unworn items.

## Tips to sell more here

1. Check the last sale and highest bid for your exact size before setting an ask, and sell to a strong bid when one appears.
2. Ship within the required window using the provided label to avoid penalties.
3. Inspect boxes, tags and items carefully before shipping, since failing verification is costly.
4. Focus on items well above $55 so the $5 minimum does not consume your margin.
5. Track your quarterly sales and volume, and plan to reach the next seller level before the quarter ends.
6. Compare StockX payouts with other resale channels for the same item before committing.
7. Package items securely, with double-boxing for sneakers, to protect the original box.

## Buyer protection in brief

StockX's main protection is its verification step: items are sent to StockX before they reach the buyer, and those that fail are not delivered. Buyers can contact support if something arrives wrong, and StockX handles the resolution rather than leaving it to buyer and seller. For sellers, the system means fewer disputes but strict standards, so ship exactly what was sold, in the condition stated.

Before shipping, compare your item carefully against the product listing, including size, colorway and box label, since a mismatch is a common reason for failing verification. Keep accessories and extra laces in the box if they came with the product. If an item fails, review StockX's explanation and your options for return, and use it to tighten your inspection routine for future sales.
