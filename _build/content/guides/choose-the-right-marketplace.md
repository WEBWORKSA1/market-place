There is no single best marketplace. There is only the best fit for a particular item, at a particular price, in a particular place, for a seller with a particular amount of time. This guide gives you a simple four-question framework so you can make that call quickly and stop guessing.

## Key takeaways

- Match the platform to the buyer who already shops there, not to the platform with the most users.
- Bulky, heavy or low-value items usually belong on local marketplaces; small, specialised items travel well.
- Higher-priced items deserve platforms with stronger buyer and seller protection, even if fees are higher.
- Availability, shipping networks and local habits differ by country, so check where a platform actually operates.
- Effort is a real cost: factor in messages, meetups and returns, not only fees.

## Start with the buyer, not the platform

Every marketplace has a personality. Some are built around search, where buyers type exactly what they want and compare listings. Others are built around browsing and social discovery, where buyers scroll a feed, follow sellers and react to style. Local classifieds are built around proximity and convenience.

Ask one question first: **where does the person who wants this item already shop?** A collector hunting for a specific camera lens searches with model numbers. A shopper looking for a vintage jacket scrolls by look and size. Someone furnishing a first apartment checks what is nearby this weekend. Put your listing where that person is looking.

## The four-question framework

### 1. What type of product is it?

Product type narrows the field faster than anything else.

| Item type | Platforms that usually fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Electronics, parts, collectibles, media | Large search-driven marketplaces such as eBay; Mercari | Buyers search by model and condition; national reach |
| Handmade goods, vintage, craft supplies | Etsy | Built for these categories, with rules on what qualifies |
| Branded and vintage clothing | Poshmark, Depop, Vinted, Mercari | Fashion-focused audiences browse by brand, size and style |
| Furniture, appliances, large items | Facebook Marketplace, Kijiji, Craigslist | Local pickup avoids freight costs and damage |
| New, branded retail products in quantity | Amazon | Catalog model suits identical new items, with stricter seller requirements |

This table is a starting point, not a rule. Plenty of clothing sells on eBay and plenty of electronics sell locally. Our [marketplace profiles](../marketplaces/index.html) cover each platform's strengths, rules and quirks in more detail.

### 2. What is the price point?

Price changes the calculation in two ways.

**Low-value items** (think a paperback or a basic phone case) often struggle on shipping platforms because postage can be a big share of the sale price. These do better in bundles, in local lots, or on platforms where buyers are used to paying shipping on top.

**High-value items** raise the stakes on fraud, returns and disputes. Here, a platform's buyer protection, seller protection, authentication programs and dispute process matter more than a small difference in fees. A local cash sale of an expensive laptop may save fees but exposes you to counterfeit cash and safety risks; see our [safe local meetups guide](../guides/safe-local-meetups.html) if you go that route.

Before you decide, put your expected sale price into the [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html) for two or three candidate platforms. The difference in what you keep is sometimes smaller than you expect, and sometimes much larger.

### 3. Where are you, and where are your buyers?

Marketplaces are not equally strong everywhere. Some are national giants in one country and barely present in the next. Kijiji, for example, is a Canadian classifieds site, while several fashion resale apps have their strongest audiences in specific countries or regions.

Check three things:

- **Does the platform operate in your country**, with payouts in your currency?
- **Does it support shipping where you are**, with integrated labels and tracking?
- **Is there a real local audience** for local pickup in your area? Search your own city and look at how many recent listings and sales you see.

Selling across borders widens your audience but adds customs forms, longer delivery times and more complicated returns. Leave it until you are comfortable with domestic sales.

### 4. How much effort can you give each sale?

Every platform asks something different of you.

- **Local classifieds** mean fast cash and no packing, but also lots of messages, no-shows and meetups.
- **Shipping marketplaces** mean packing and post office runs, but buyers can find you from anywhere.
- **Social resale apps** reward frequent activity such as sharing, refreshing and responding to offers.
- **Catalog-style marketplaces** involve more setup, account requirements and policy compliance, which suits sellers moving larger volumes.

Be honest about your time. A platform that pays slightly more per sale but demands constant attention may earn you less per hour.

## A quick decision walkthrough

Use these steps for any item:

1. **Search sold listings** for the item on two or three candidate platforms. Note how many sold recently and at what prices.
2. **Weigh and measure it** (or estimate). If it is large or heavy, lean local.
3. **Estimate net proceeds** on each platform using the [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html), including realistic shipping.
4. **Consider risk.** For high-value or often-counterfeited items, favour platforms with strong protection and authentication.
5. **Consider effort.** Pick the option that gives the best result for the time you have.
6. **List, then review after two weeks.** If views are poor, the platform may be wrong, not just the price.

## What to check in a platform's policies

Two marketplaces can look similar on the surface and treat sellers very differently once something goes wrong. Before you commit a batch of listings, spend fifteen minutes reading the seller policies. Look for:

- **Prohibited and restricted items.** Some platforms ban or limit categories such as cosmetics, used electronics with batteries, replicas or certain brands.
- **Return rules.** Find out whether returns are optional, required for some reasons, or handled by the platform, and who pays return postage.
- **Seller protection.** Note what you must do to qualify, such as using platform labels, tracked shipping or signature confirmation above certain values.
- **Payout timing.** New accounts often have funds held for a period. Know this before you rely on the money.
- **Account standards.** Late shipping, cancellations and unanswered messages can limit your account on some platforms.

## Worked examples

**A mid-century side table, collected locally only.** It is bulky and could be damaged in transit, so local classifieds are the natural first stop. Good photos and a clear description of dimensions help it stand out.

**A discontinued camera lens.** The right buyer might be hundreds of kilometres away and will search by exact model number. A large, search-driven marketplace with national reach usually wins, even after shipping.

**A bag of children's clothing in mixed sizes.** Individually, each piece is worth little. Sold as a size-specific bundle on a fashion resale app or as a local lot, it moves faster and saves on postage.

**A designer handbag.** High value and a common target for counterfeits. A platform with authentication options and solid buyer and seller protection is worth the cost, both for reaching serious buyers and for resolving disputes.

## When to add a second marketplace

Once you are listing regularly on one platform, adding a second can widen your reach. The usual signs that it is time:

- You have items that keep getting views but no sales, and a different audience might suit them.
- You have built a repeatable photo and listing process.
- You can track inventory reliably, so a sale on one platform triggers removal everywhere else.

Selling the same item twice and cancelling an order hurts your account standing, so do not cross-list until you have a system. ## Common mistakes when choosing a platform

- **Following the crowd.** The biggest platform is not automatically the best one for your item.
- **Comparing headline fees only.** Payment processing, shipping, promoted listing costs and currency conversion all affect what you keep.
- **Ignoring category rules.** Some platforms restrict or ban certain brands, item types or conditions. Check before you list.
- **Giving up too soon.** A week without sales is rarely enough data. Review views, saves and offers before switching.
