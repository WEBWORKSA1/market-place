A great item with a weak listing is nearly invisible. Marketplace search is a matching game between the words buyers type and the words in your title, description and item details. Once a buyer finds you, the description has one job: answer their questions honestly so they feel safe clicking buy.

## Key takeaways

- Write titles in the words buyers actually search, starting with brand and item type.
- Fill in every item specific the platform offers; they power search filters.
- Lead the description with the facts a buyer needs, then add details and context.
- Describe condition precisely and show every flaw in the photos.
- Avoid keyword stuffing, misleading brand mentions and filler words.

## How marketplace search finds your listing

Each platform's search works differently, and most do not publish exactly how they rank results. But the broad principles are consistent. Search looks for listings whose titles, categories and item specifics match the buyer's query. Filters such as size, brand, condition and price then narrow the results. Many platforms also weigh signals like recent activity, seller performance, price competitiveness and shipping speed.

You control the first part entirely. The right words in the right fields get you into the results. The rest of this guide is about choosing those words and then turning views into sales.

## Writing titles that match real searches

### Find the words buyers use

Start typing your item into the marketplace search bar and note the autocomplete suggestions. Those are common searches. Then look at sold listings for the same item and note which words appear in their titles. Buyers rarely search for "lovely blue top"; they search for "Patagonia fleece women's medium".

### Use a simple title formula

For most items, this order works well:

**Brand + item type + model or style + key attributes (size, colour, material) + condition or notable feature**

Examples:

- "Sony WH-1000XM4 Wireless Noise Cancelling Headphones Black with Case"
- "Levi's 501 Original Fit Jeans Men's 32x30 Dark Wash"
- "Le Creuset Round Dutch Oven 4.2 L Cerise Red Enamel Cast Iron"
- "Vintage 1970s Pyrex Butterfly Gold Mixing Bowl Set of 3"

### Title do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use the exact brand and model name | Write "not Nike" or "like Apple" to borrow a brand's search traffic |
| Include size, colour and material | Use all caps or symbols like L@@K or !!! |
| Use common spellings and abbreviations buyers use | Waste space on "rare", "wow" or "beautiful" |
| Add notable features such as "with box" or "unlocked" | Stuff unrelated keywords that do not describe the item |
| Match the item's real condition | Claim "new" for anything that has been used |

Mentioning other brands to draw search traffic is a policy violation on many platforms and can get listings removed. It also wastes the buyer's time, which is not how you build a reputation.

## Item specifics: the hidden half of search

Item specifics (sometimes called attributes, tags or details) are structured fields like brand, size, colour, material, style, model number and condition. Platforms use them to sort and filter results.

A buyer who filters for "size M" and "brand: Arc'teryx" will never see your jacket if those fields are blank, even if your title says it. So:

1. **Fill in every field that applies**, not only the required ones.
2. **Use the platform's own options** from dropdowns where available, instead of custom text.
3. **Be accurate.** Wrong specifics lead to returns and frustrated buyers.
4. **Choose the most specific category.** A listing in the wrong category may miss filters entirely.

## Writing the description

Buyers skim. Put the most important information at the top, in short lines, and save the story for the end.

### A description template that works

1. **One-line summary.** What it is, the condition and the most important fact. "Sony WH-1000XM4 headphones, fully working, light wear on the headband, includes original case and cable."
2. **Key details.** Model, size or measurements, colour, material, year or edition.
3. **Condition, in specifics.** What works, what has been tested, every flaw and where it is.
4. **What is included.** Accessories, box, manuals, chargers. Also what is not included.
5. **Shipping and handling.** How you pack and how quickly you ship.
6. **Optional context.** Why you are selling, how it was used, or how it fits with other items you sell.

### Measurements beat sizes

Sizes vary between brands and over time. For clothing, give flat measurements such as chest, length, waist and inseam. For furniture and decor, give height, width and depth. For bags, include strap drop. Measurements answer the question buyers message about most often.

## Condition: be precise and be honest

Condition language is where trust is won or lost. Vague words like "good" or "used" mean different things to different people. Precise descriptions mean the same thing to everyone.

Compare:

- **Vague:** "Good condition, some wear."
- **Precise:** "Good used condition. Light scuffing on both toe caps (photos 5 and 6), small pen mark inside the left lining (photo 8). Soles have plenty of tread. No odours, from a non-smoking home."

Use your platform's official condition grades, then describe the reality underneath them. If you have not tested something, say so rather than implying it works. "Untested, sold as is for parts or repair" is honest; "should work fine" is not.

Accurate condition notes also protect you. If a buyer claims an item was not as described, your clear description and matching photos are your strongest evidence. Our guide on [how sellers avoid scams](../guides/avoid-seller-scams.html) explains how this helps in disputes.

## Writing for buyers who are deciding

Once the facts are covered, a few small touches improve conversions.

- **Answer the common questions in advance.** If buyers keep asking whether a phone is unlocked, add the answer to every phone listing.
- **Use plain language.** Short sentences and line breaks are easier to read on a phone.
- **Mention bundle options.** "Ask about combined shipping with other items in my shop" can turn one sale into three.
- **Stay professional.** Avoid negative lines like "no lowballers" or "don't waste my time". They make you look difficult and deter reasonable buyers too.

## Before and after: rewriting a weak listing

Here is how the principles above change a typical first attempt.

**Before**

Title: "Nice jacket!! Great for winter L@@K"

Description: "Selling my jacket, barely worn, good condition. Size L. Smoke free home. No lowballers."

This listing will struggle. The title contains no brand, no item type a buyer would search, no size and no colour. The description leaves out measurements, material and any detail about wear, and the last line puts off reasonable buyers.

**After**

Title: "The North Face Men's Thermoball Insulated Jacket Large Black Full Zip"

Description:

- The North Face men's Thermoball insulated jacket, size Large, black.
- Worn one season. Very good used condition: light pilling on the left cuff (photo 7), all zips work smoothly, no stains or tears.
- Measurements laid flat: chest 61 cm armpit to armpit, length 74 cm from collar to hem, sleeve 66 cm.
- Shell and lining as shown on the care label (photo 9).
- Packed carefully and shipped within one business day. Happy to answer questions or combine shipping with other items in my shop.

The revised version gives search engines the brand, item type, gender, size and colour, fills the gaps buyers usually message about, and discloses the one flaw clearly. It is also more pleasant to read. The measurements above are only an illustration; always measure your own item.

## A listing checklist before you publish

Run through this every time:

1. Title starts with brand and item type and uses words buyers search.
2. Category is the most specific one available.
3. Every relevant item specific is filled in.
4. The first line of the description summarises the item and its condition.
5. Measurements or dimensions are included.
6. Every flaw is described and shown in a photo.
7. What is included, and not included, is listed.
8. Price is based on sold comps and checked with the [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html).
9. Shipping weight and size are accurate, so postage is right.
10. Spelling of brand and model names is correct.
