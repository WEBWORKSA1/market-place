Pricing is where most of your profit is won or lost. Set the price too high and an item sits for months; set it too low and you hand your margin to the first buyer who notices. A good price comes from evidence, a clear view of your costs and a plan for offers before they arrive.

## Key takeaways

- Price from **sold** listings, not active ones. Asking prices show hope; sold prices show demand.
- Adjust comps for condition, completeness, season and how quickly you need the cash.
- Calculate your net after fees and shipping before choosing a price, not after the sale.
- Decide your lowest acceptable offer in advance and counter with a reason.
- Use bundles to move low-value items and to raise the average value of each order.

## Why sold listings beat asking prices

Anyone can list a used jacket for any price. That tells you nothing about whether a buyer will pay it. Sold listings tell you what real buyers actually paid, recently, for comparable items.

Most large marketplaces let you filter search results to show sold or completed items. Where that filter is not available, look at listings marked as sold or reserved, check third-party price guides for collectibles, and note how long active listings have been sitting. A listing that has been live for months at a high price is a warning, not a benchmark.

## How to find good comps

A comp (short for comparable) is a recent sale of an item close enough to yours that the price is meaningful. Good comps match on the things that drive value.

1. **Search precisely.** Use brand, model, size, edition or colour, not a generic description.
2. **Filter to sold items** and, where possible, sort by most recent.
3. **Match condition.** New with tags, like new, good and for parts are different markets.
4. **Match completeness.** Original box, accessories, manuals, chargers and dust bags all affect value.
5. **Collect five to ten comps** if they exist, and note the low, middle and high.
6. **Throw out outliers.** One unusually high sale may have been a bidding war; one very low sale may have had hidden damage.

Note shipping too. Two items that each sold at the same headline price are not equal if one charged the buyer for postage and the other included it.

## Adjust for the details that move price

Once you have a middle value, adjust it.

| Factor | Usually pushes price up | Usually pushes price down |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | New, unworn, fully tested | Visible wear, untested, missing parts |
| Completeness | Original box, accessories, receipts | Loose item, generic charger |
| Season | Coats in autumn, swimwear in spring | Selling out of season |
| Scarcity | Discontinued, limited, hard-to-find size | Common model, many active listings |
| Your photos and description | Clear, detailed, honest | Dark, blurry or vague |

The last row is the one you control most. Strong photos and a precise description can justify a price at the top of your comp range. See our guides on [product photos](../guides/product-photos.html) and [writing listings that sell](../guides/write-listings-that-sell.html).

## Do the fee math before you list

Your sale price is not what you keep. Depending on the platform, deductions can include a selling fee, a payment processing fee, optional promoted listing costs, currency conversion and, if you offer free shipping, the label itself.

Work backwards from the amount you want to receive:

1. Estimate the packed weight and size of the item and the shipping cost.
2. Enter your intended price into our [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html) for your platform.
3. Subtract packaging costs, even if small.
4. Compare the net amount with what you paid for the item, if you bought it to resell.

Fee structures change and differ by category and country, so check your platform's current fee page or our [marketplace profiles](../marketplaces/index.html) rather than relying on memory. If the net looks thin on one platform, the [Marketplace Match quiz](../tools/marketplace-match.html) can point you to platforms that may suit the item better.

## Choose your strategy: speed or value

Every pricing decision balances how fast you want the sale against how much you want to earn.

- **Price to move.** Set the price near the low-to-middle of recent comps. Use this for items you need gone, common items with many competing listings, and when you are building early reviews.
- **Price for value.** Set the price near the top of your comps and be patient. Use this for scarce items, excellent condition, complete sets and items where your listing is clearly better than the competition.
- **Price to negotiate.** Set a price somewhat above your target and expect offers. This suits platforms and categories where buyers routinely haggle.

### Auctions versus fixed price

Some marketplaces let you choose between an auction and a fixed price. Auctions can work well when demand is strong and buyers compete, such as for collectibles, rare items or anything with an active community of fans. They let the market set the price, which is useful when comps are thin.

The trade-off is control. An auction that attracts only one or two bidders may end well below the item's value. If you use auctions, start them at a price you would be content to accept, check whether your platform allows a reserve price, and time the end for when your buyers are likely to be browsing. For common items with plenty of sold comps, fixed price with offers enabled is usually simpler and more predictable.

### Selling multiples of the same item

If you have several identical items, list them as one listing with a quantity, where the platform allows it, rather than many separate listings. Watch how quickly the first few sell. Fast sales suggest you can hold or raise the price for the rest; slow sales suggest a small reduction.

Whatever you choose, write down your **floor**: the lowest offer you will accept. Deciding in advance stops you from accepting a lowball offer just to end an awkward conversation.

## Handling offers without losing margin

Offers are information. A flood of low offers suggests your price is above what the market will bear. No offers at all on a well-viewed listing suggests the same, or a problem with the photos or description.

### A simple offer playbook

- **Offer at or above your floor:** accept, or counter slightly higher if it is early in the listing's life.
- **Offer modestly below your floor:** counter at or just above your floor and give a reason, such as condition or included accessories.
- **Very low offer:** counter once at a fair price, or decline politely. Do not take it personally; some buyers send low offers to everyone.
- **Repeat interest from the same buyer:** consider sending a targeted offer if your platform supports it, since that buyer has already shown interest.

Keep all negotiation inside the platform's messaging and offer tools. A buyer who wants to finalise the deal by text or another app is a red flag; our guide to [avoiding seller scams](../guides/avoid-seller-scams.html) explains why.

## Bundles: the underused pricing tool

Bundling is how you sell items that are not worth shipping on their own, and how you increase the value of each sale.

- **Themed bundles** such as a set of paperbacks by one author, a size-specific lot of children's clothing, or a cable and accessory kit appeal to buyers who want everything at once.
- **Bundle discounts** encourage buyers to add a second or third item from your shop. Many platforms let you set a discount automatically.
- **Shipping savings** are the real win. One parcel with three items often costs far less than three separate parcels, and you can share that saving with the buyer.

Price a bundle by adding the individual values, subtracting a discount that still leaves you ahead after combined shipping and fees, and making the saving obvious in the title or first line of the description.

## Refreshing stale listings

If an item has not sold after a couple of weeks, work through this list before cutting the price deeply:

1. Re-check sold comps. The market may have moved.
2. Replace the main photo with a cleaner, brighter shot.
3. Rewrite the title with the keywords buyers use.
4. Make sure all item specifics are filled in.
5. Lower the price modestly, especially if your platform alerts watchers to price drops.
6. Consider a bundle or a different marketplace.

A price is a hypothesis. Test it, read the response and adjust. Over a few months, your own sales history becomes your best set of comps, which is one more reason to keep good records, as covered in our [records and taxes guide](../guides/seller-taxes-records.html).
