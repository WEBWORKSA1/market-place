Reselling, or flipping, means buying items for less than they will sell for and pocketing the difference. It sounds simple, and at its core it is. What separates a profitable reselling business from a garage full of unsold stock is discipline: buying with data, tracking every item and building systems before volume overwhelms you.

## Key takeaways

- Buy based on recent sold prices and your full costs, not on hunches or asking prices.
- Specialise in a few categories you know well before branching out.
- Give every item a SKU and a storage location from the moment you buy it.
- Cross-list only once you can reliably remove items everywhere when they sell.
- Track costs and income from day one; you will need the records for tax and decisions.

## Decide what kind of reseller you want to be

Resellers tend to fall into a few broad models, and each needs a different mix of skills and capital.

| Model | What it involves | Strengths | Challenges |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thrift and secondhand | Sourcing used items from thrift stores, yard sales, estate sales | Low cost per item, wide variety | Time-intensive, every item is unique |
| Retail arbitrage | Buying discounted new items from stores to resell | Identical items are easy to list | Brand and platform restrictions; competitive |
| Liquidation and returns | Buying pallets or lots of returned or overstock goods | Volume at low unit cost | Unknown condition; testing and disposal costs |
| Niche specialist | Focusing on one category, like vintage denim or camera gear | Deep knowledge, loyal buyers | Smaller sourcing pool |

Many successful resellers start with thrift and secondhand, then specialise as they learn what sells. If you are brand new to selling, start with our [first-month selling plan](../guides/sell-online-beginners.html) using items you already own.

## Sourcing with data, not hope

The money in reselling is made when you buy. Paying too much for an item is the most common mistake, and no amount of good listing can fix it.

### The buy-or-pass check

Before buying, look up the item on your phone:

1. **Search sold listings** for the exact item on the platform where you plan to sell.
2. **Note how many have sold recently.** A high price that rarely sells may not be worth chasing.
3. **Estimate your net:** expected sale price minus platform fees, payment fees, shipping, packaging and the purchase price. Use the [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html) for the platform side.
4. **Consider your time.** Cleaning, testing, photographing and listing all take effort.
5. **Decide your minimum margin** in advance, and pass on anything that does not meet it.

### Where resellers source

- Thrift and charity shops
- Yard, garage and estate sales
- Local classifieds and free listings
- Clearance racks and end-of-season sales
- Auctions, both online and in person
- Liquidation and returns sellers

Whatever you source, stay within the rules. Many platforms restrict certain brands, product types, recalled items and counterfeit goods. Selling fakes, even unknowingly, can get your account suspended and may break the law. If you are unsure about an item's authenticity, pass.

## Build an inventory system early

Ten items fit in your memory. A hundred do not. Set up a system before you need it.

### Processing: clean, test and grade

Every item you buy needs processing before it can be listed, and this step is where much of your added value comes from. Set a standard routine:

- **Clean** according to the material: lint-roll and steam clothing, wipe down electronics, polish hard goods. A clean item photographs better and earns better reviews.
- **Test** everything that has a function. Power on electronics, check zips, buttons and clasps, try every setting. Note anything that does not work.
- **Grade** condition using your platform's categories, and write down specific flaws while the item is in front of you.
- **Measure** and record dimensions or garment measurements.
- **Decide** whether the item is ready to list, needs a small repair, or should be set aside as a loss.

Doing this once, thoroughly, saves you from answering the same questions later and from returns caused by details you missed.

### SKUs and locations

Give every item a SKU (stock-keeping unit), a short code you can write on a tag or bag. Keep it simple, for example the category initials and a number, plus the storage bin. Record the SKU in the listing's private notes field if your platform has one, so when something sells you know exactly where to find it.

### Storage

- Store items in labelled bins or on shelves numbered by location.
- Keep clothing folded in clear bags to protect it from dust and damp.
- Separate "to list", "listed" and "sold, to ship" areas so nothing gets lost.

### Your inventory spreadsheet

At minimum, track:

- SKU and item description
- Date and place purchased
- Purchase cost
- Platforms listed on
- Date sold, sale price and platform
- Fees, shipping cost and net profit
- Storage location

This record doubles as your business ledger, which is covered in more depth in our [records and taxes guide](../guides/seller-taxes-records.html).

## Cross-listing without overselling

Cross-listing means listing the same item on multiple platforms to reach more buyers. It can work well, especially for unique items that might suit different audiences. Our guide on [choosing the right marketplace](../guides/choose-the-right-marketplace.html) explains which audiences different platforms attract.

The risk is selling the same item twice. Cancelling an order because the item is gone frustrates buyers and can hurt your account metrics.

**Ways to manage it:**

- **Manual delisting:** remove the item from every other platform as soon as it sells. Works at low volume if you are disciplined.
- **Cross-listing tools:** third-party services can copy listings between platforms and, in some cases, delist automatically. Check each tool's terms and whether the marketplaces you use allow or support them.
- **Platform-specific stock:** keep some items exclusive to one platform where they sell best.

Adapt each listing to each platform. Titles, categories and item specifics work differently, and copying a listing without adjusting it can hurt search visibility. See [writing listings that sell](../guides/write-listings-that-sell.html).

## Build a weekly workflow

Batch similar tasks together. A typical part-time week might look like this:

1. **Sourcing:** one or two trips, or a set time for online sourcing.
2. **Processing:** clean, test, measure and assign SKUs to everything you bought.
3. **Photography:** shoot everything in one session using the setup in [product photos that sell](../guides/product-photos.html).
4. **Listing:** write and publish in batches.
5. **Daily:** answer messages, handle offers, pack and ship orders.
6. **Weekly review:** update your spreadsheet, refresh stale listings and check cash flow.

Shipping is the task that most often slips as volume grows. Our [shipping guide](../guides/shipping-101.html) has a per-order checklist that keeps it consistent.

## Know your numbers

Revenue is not profit. Review these monthly:

- **Net profit** after every cost, including supplies and mileage if you track it.
- **Sell-through rate:** what share of the items you listed actually sold in a given period.
- **Average days to sell** for each category.
- **Return rate** and reasons for returns.
- **Dead stock:** items unsold for a long time that tie up cash and space.

These numbers show you which categories to source more of and which to drop. Clear out dead stock regularly through price drops, bundles, local lots or donation. Cash in your pocket is worth more than an item on a shelf. The [pricing strategy guide](../guides/pricing-strategy.html) covers how to refresh stale listings.

## Scaling sensibly

Once the basics work, growth is about removing bottlenecks one at a time.

- **Reinvest profits gradually** rather than borrowing to buy big lots.
- **Improve your space** with more shelving, a dedicated packing station and bulk supplies.
- **Specialise further** in the categories with the best margins and fastest sales.
- **Add a platform** once your inventory system can handle it.
- **Consider help** for time-consuming tasks such as photography or packing, and look into local rules before hiring.
- **Formalise the business:** depending on where you live, that may mean registering a business, collecting sales taxes, getting insurance or opening a separate bank account.

Many platforms now report seller income to tax authorities, and the rules differ by country. Read [records and taxes for online sellers](../guides/seller-taxes-records.html) and get advice from a qualified tax professional as your volume grows.

Finally, protect what you have built. A single account suspension or a run of scams can stall a reselling business. Follow the habits in [how sellers avoid scams and fraud](../guides/avoid-seller-scams.html), and keep good relationships with your buyers.
