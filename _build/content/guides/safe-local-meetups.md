Local selling avoids shipping costs and gets cash in hand quickly, but it brings you face to face with strangers. The vast majority of meetups are uneventful, and a few simple habits keep it that way. This guide covers where to meet, how to handle payment and what to do differently for expensive items.

## Key takeaways

- Meet in busy, public, well-lit places with cameras, during daylight when possible.
- Tell someone where you are going, who you are meeting and when you expect to be back.
- Confirm payment is real before handing over an item, whether cash or transfer.
- For high-value items, choose stronger safeguards such as a bank lobby meetup.
- Trust your instincts: you can cancel or walk away at any point.

## Before the meetup: set it up safely

Most safety work happens before you leave home.

**Keep communication on the platform** for as long as possible. Messages inside the marketplace leave a record linked to the other person's account. Be careful about giving out your personal phone number; if you need to coordinate on the day, some sellers use a secondary number.

**Check the other person's profile.** Look at account age, reviews and whether their messages make sense. A brand-new account with no history is not automatically a problem, but it is a reason to be a little more careful.

**Agree on the details in writing:** item, price, payment method, meeting place and time. This avoids surprises and gives you a record.

**Tell someone you trust.** Share the meeting location, time, the other person's username and a screenshot of the listing. Tell them when you expect to check in afterwards.

## Choosing where to meet

The location does most of the work. A good meetup spot is public, busy, well lit, covered by cameras and easy to leave.

| Location | Good for | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|
| Designated safe exchange zone | Most local deals | Not available everywhere; check if your police or municipality offers one |
| Bank lobby during business hours | Cash deals and high-value items | Ask whether the branch allows it; staff and cameras present |
| Busy shopping centre entrance or café | Small to medium items | Crowded at peak times; choose a spot near staff |
| Well-lit store car park in daylight | Larger items needing a vehicle | Park close to the entrance and cameras |
| Your home | Only for bulky items that cannot travel | Move item to the entrance or garage; have another adult present |

Some police services and municipalities designate safe exchange zones, often in a station car park or lobby with camera coverage. Search your city's name with "safe exchange zone" to see if one exists near you.

## Handling payment safely

Agree the payment method before you meet, and do not let anyone change it on the spot.

### Cash

Cash cannot be reversed once you have it, which is why many local sellers prefer it. The main risk is counterfeit notes.

- Count the money in front of the buyer before handing over the item.
- Learn the security features of your local currency. Central banks publish guides on how to check banknotes.
- For larger amounts, meet at the buyer's bank so they can withdraw cash in front of you, or so you can deposit it on the spot.

### Electronic transfers and payment apps

Bank transfers and payment apps avoid carrying cash, but scammers use fake notification emails and texts that look like a completed transfer.

- Open your bank or payment app yourself and confirm the money has arrived, not just that a payment is "pending".
- Ignore screenshots and emails as proof.
- Be aware that some peer-to-peer payment apps offer little or no protection for purchases between strangers, and some transfers can be reversed in certain circumstances.

### What to avoid

- Cheques and money orders from strangers, which can bounce or be forged.
- Any offer to pay more than the asking price with a request to return the difference. That is an overpayment scam, described in [how sellers avoid scams](../guides/avoid-seller-scams.html).
- Requests to pay or be paid with gift cards or cryptocurrency.

## High-value items need extra safeguards

Phones, laptops, jewellery, designer goods, bikes, tools and vehicles attract more scams and more risk. Raise your standards accordingly.

1. **Choose the safest location available,** ideally a bank lobby or safe exchange zone.
2. **Bring a friend** if you can.
3. **Verify the buyer or seller** a little more: ask questions, check their profile history, and confirm they are bringing the agreed payment method.
4. **For buyers: test the item thoroughly.** Power on electronics, check serial or IMEI numbers, confirm a phone is not locked to someone else's account, and make sure accessories are as described.
5. **For sellers: clear personal data** from devices and sign out of accounts before the meetup, but do not hand over the item until payment is confirmed.
6. **Keep a simple written receipt** with date, item, serial number and price, signed by both parties, for expensive items.

For vehicles, follow your local rules for transferring ownership and checking whether the vehicle has outstanding debt or has been reported stolen. Those checks vary by country and province or state.

## During the meetup

- **Arrive a little early** and look around. If anything feels wrong, leave.
- **Stay in public view.** Do not get into the other person's car or invite them into yours.
- **Bring only what you need:** the item, your phone and a way to receive payment. Leave unnecessary valuables at home.
- **Let buyers inspect,** but stay with the item. Do not let anyone walk off to "test" it.
- **Keep it short and polite.** Long, wandering negotiations can be a distraction tactic.
- **Do not renegotiate under pressure.** If someone tries to cut the agreed price at the last minute, you can say no and leave.

### Large items and home pickups

Furniture, appliances and gym equipment often cannot be carried to a café. If a buyer must collect from your home, reduce what they see and where they go.

- Move the item to a garage, porch, driveway or just inside the front door before they arrive.
- Have another adult at home, and let a trusted contact know the pickup time.
- Do not leave keys, mail, packages or valuables in view.
- Agree in advance who will lift and carry, and whether the buyer is bringing help and a suitable vehicle.
- Take payment before the item leaves your property.

Buyers collecting large items should apply the same thinking in reverse: bring a friend, share the address with someone you trust, and inspect the item in good light before loading it. If the seller's address or story changes at the last minute, rearrange or cancel.

## Red flags that mean "walk away"

1. The person insists on a remote or private location.
2. They change the meeting place or time repeatedly, especially at the last minute.
3. They will not use the platform's messages or refuse to share basic details.
4. They want to pay with a method you did not agree on.
5. They send someone else in their place without telling you in advance.
6. They pressure you to hurry, or try to separate you from your item.
7. Their story does not add up, or their account seems to have been created that day.

You do not owe anyone an explanation for cancelling. Your safety matters more than any sale.

## Your local meetup checklist

**Before:**

- Messages and agreement kept on the platform.
- Profile checked.
- Location chosen: public, busy, cameras, daylight.
- Payment method agreed.
- A trusted contact knows the plan.

**At the meetup:**

- Arrived early and in public view.
- Item inspected (buyer) or kept within reach (seller).
- Payment confirmed in hand or in your own banking app.
- Item handed over only after payment is confirmed.

**After:**

- Check-in sent to your trusted contact.
- Listing marked as sold.
- Receipt or record saved for your [seller records](../guides/seller-taxes-records.html).
