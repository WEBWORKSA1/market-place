Your first month of selling online decides whether this becomes a useful habit or a pile of half-finished listings. The good news is that the work is learnable, and most of it comes down to a handful of repeatable steps. This plan breaks the month into four weeks so you build skills in the right order, with a real sale at the end of each stage.

## Key takeaways

- Start with 10 to 20 items you already own and understand, not inventory you bought.
- Set up your account, payout method and security before you list anything.
- Price from sold listings, not asking prices, and work out your fees before you commit.
- Ship within the time you promise and communicate early; early reviews matter most.
- Spend the last week reviewing what sold and what did not, then build a weekly routine.

## Before you begin: what you actually need

You do not need special equipment. A recent phone with a decent camera, a well-lit spot near a window, a tape measure and a kitchen or postal scale cover almost everything. Add a stash of clean boxes, padded mailers and packing tape from household recycling and you are ready.

What you do need is a little preparation:

- **A dedicated email address** for selling, so platform notices do not get lost in personal mail.
- **Two-factor authentication** on your selling account and on that email address.
- **A payout method** linked and verified before your first sale, so funds are not held up.
- **A simple spreadsheet** with columns for item, date listed, price, sale price, fees, shipping cost and profit.

If you are unsure where to sell, read [how to choose the right marketplace](../guides/choose-the-right-marketplace.html) first. For a first month, one platform is plenty. Cross-listing comes later.

## Week 1: Set up and choose your first items

Spend this week getting your account right and picking a starter batch. Fill in your profile completely, add a clear photo or logo, and write a short, friendly bio that says what you sell and how fast you ship. Buyers check profiles more often than you might expect, especially on new accounts with no reviews.

Next, walk through your home and pull 15 to 25 candidates. Good starter items share a few traits:

- You know what they are, including brand, model or size.
- They work and are clean, or you can describe flaws precisely.
- They are small, sturdy and light enough to ship cheaply.
- Similar items have actually sold recently on your chosen platform.

Search each item on your marketplace and filter to **sold or completed** listings where the platform allows it. If nothing similar has sold in the past few months, set the item aside. Your goal this month is learning through sales, not testing obscure items.

### Week 1 checklist

1. Create or tidy your selling account and turn on two-factor authentication.
2. Verify identity and payout details if the platform asks.
3. Read the platform's rules on prohibited items, returns and shipping times.
4. Gather 15 to 25 candidate items and research sold prices for each.
5. Keep the 10 to 20 with the clearest demand.

## Week 2: Photograph and list your first batch

This is the heaviest week, so batch the work. Photograph everything in one session, then write listings in another. Switching between tasks wastes more time than you would think.

For photos, use daylight, a plain background and at least five shots per item: front, back, sides, labels or model numbers, and any flaws up close. Our guide to [product photos that sell](../guides/product-photos.html) covers lighting and angles in detail.

For listings, put the words a buyer would actually type into the title: brand, item type, model, size, color and key feature. Fill in every item specific the platform offers, since those fields feed search filters. Be blunt about condition. A buyer who receives exactly what they expected leaves a good review; a buyer who finds an undisclosed scratch opens a return. See [writing titles and descriptions that get found](../guides/write-listings-that-sell.html) for a full template.

### Pricing your first items

Look at what identical or near-identical items sold for, adjust for condition, and decide whether you want speed or top value. For a first batch, pricing slightly below the middle of recent sales helps you get reviews quickly. Before you lock in a price, run the numbers through our [fee calculator](../tools/fee-calculator.html) so you know what you will keep after platform and payment fees. The [pricing strategy guide](../guides/pricing-strategy.html) goes deeper on offers and bundles.

## Week 3: Handle messages, offers and your first shipments

Once listings are live, questions arrive. Answer within a few hours where you can. Quick, polite replies win sales and they also signal to some platforms that you are an active seller.

Expect offers, many of them low. Decide in advance the lowest price you will accept for each item and write it in your spreadsheet. Then you can counter calmly instead of reacting in the moment.

When something sells:

1. Confirm payment shows inside the platform, never just in an email. Fake payment emails are a common scam, covered in [how sellers avoid scams](../guides/avoid-seller-scams.html).
2. Pack the item so it cannot move inside the box, with padding on all sides.
3. Weigh and measure the packed parcel and buy the label through the platform when possible, so tracking uploads automatically.
4. Ship within your stated handling time, and send the buyer a short note when it is on its way.

For packing details, read [shipping and packing for marketplace sellers](../guides/shipping-101.html). If you are selling locally instead, follow the [safe local meetup checklist](../guides/safe-local-meetups.html).

### Reviews and the occasional problem

Your first handful of reviews carry extra weight because buyers have nothing else to judge you by. You cannot buy them, but you can earn them: describe items accurately, ship on time, and send a brief thank-you once the parcel arrives. Some platforms let sellers leave feedback for buyers too, which often prompts a reply in kind.

If a buyer reports a problem, stay calm and keep the conversation inside the platform's messages. Ask for photos, check them against your own listing photos, and follow the platform's return or dispute process rather than improvising a side deal. A fair, quick resolution on a small item is often worth more to a new account than the item itself.

## Week 4: Review results and build a routine

By now you should have some sales, some unsold listings and a lot of new knowledge. Spend an hour reviewing.

For items that sold, check your actual profit after fees and shipping. Some items that looked worthwhile may have earned very little once postage was counted. That is a valuable lesson about what to skip next time.

For items that did not sell, look at views and saves where the platform shows them:

| What you see | Likely cause | What to try |
|---|---|---|
| Few views | Title or category is off, or demand is low | Rewrite the title with better keywords; check the category |
| Many views, no offers | Price is high or photos raise doubts | Compare against sold listings again; reshoot the main photo |
| Offers but no sale | Gap between your floor and buyers' expectations | Accept a fair counter or offer a bundle |
| Questions about the same detail | Description is missing information | Add the answer to the listing itself |

Then set a weekly rhythm. Many part-time sellers use one session for sourcing or decluttering, one for photos and listing, and short daily slots for messages and shipping. Consistency matters more than volume.

## Mistakes that slow new sellers down

- **Listing too much at once.** Fifty listings and no packing system leads to late shipments and poor reviews.
- **Pricing from asking prices.** Active listings show what sellers hope for. Sold listings show what buyers paid.
- **Ignoring fees and postage.** A low-value item can cost more to send than it earns.
- **Vague condition notes.** "Good condition" means nothing. "Light scuff on the left corner, see photo 6" builds trust.
- **Taking deals off the platform.** Requests to pay outside the app or chat by text are a classic scam setup and usually remove your seller protection.
- **Skipping records.** Even casual sellers benefit from knowing what they earned. The [records and taxes guide](../guides/seller-taxes-records.html) explains what to keep.

## Where to go after month one

If you enjoyed the process and your numbers look healthy, you have two paths. You can keep clearing personal items as a side income, or you can start buying stock to resell. The second path is a real small business with its own skills, from sourcing to inventory management.
