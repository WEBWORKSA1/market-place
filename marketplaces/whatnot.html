Whatnot: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

A live-stream shopping app where sellers auction cards, collectibles and fashion to buyers in real time.

At a glance

  • Sellers host live video shows and run quick auctions or fixed-price sales during the stream.
  • Strong in trading cards, sports cards, comics, toys, coins, sneakers and vintage fashion.
  • Commission is 8% of the sale price in most categories, with Coins & Money at 4%.
  • Payment processing is 2.9% + $0.30 on the full order value, including shipping and tax.
  • No fees to create or manage listings; giveaways carry no seller fees, though you pay the shipping.

Who it's best for

Whatnot is built for sellers who enjoy performing and building a community. Card breakers, collectible shops, comic dealers and vintage clothing sellers use it to move large volumes in a single session, often with auctions that start low and climb. If you have a steady supply of inventory and can show up on a regular schedule, the live format can produce fast sales and repeat buyers.

It is less suitable for sellers who want to list items and walk away, or who are uncomfortable on camera. Standalone listings exist, but the platform's energy is in live shows.

The fee structure also favors certain sellers. Coin and currency dealers pay half the usual commission, and sellers of high-value comics, trading cards, sports singles and toys benefit from the limited-time 0% rate on the part of an order above $1,500. For a $3,000 card, that means commission on only the first $1,500.

What it costs to sell

There are two main fees. The commission is a percentage of the final sale price, not including shipping or taxes. In most categories it is 8%. Coins & Money pay 4%. For a limited time, Comics, Trading Card Games, Sports Singles, and Toys & Hobbies pay 8% on the first $1,500 of an order and 0% on any portion above that, with the same structure at 4% for Coins & Money.

The second fee is payment processing: 2.9% of the total order value plus $0.30 per order. Unlike the commission, processing is calculated on everything the buyer pays, including shipping and sales tax.

Worked example ($50 item, most categories, no shipping or tax counted):

  • Commission: 8% × $50 = $4.00
  • Payment processing: (2.9% × $50) + $0.30 = $1.45 + $0.30 = $1.75
  • Total fees: $4.00 + $1.75 = $5.75
  • You receive: $50.00 − $5.75 = $44.25.

In practice, buyer-paid shipping and tax increase the processing portion slightly. For example, with $6 shipping on the same order, processing would be 2.9% × $56 + $0.30 = $1.92.

Strengths

  • Fast sell-through. A good show can move dozens of items in an hour.
  • Engaged community. Viewers chat, follow and return for regular shows.
  • Price discovery. Auctions help find market value for collectibles.
  • Moderate fees. Around 11.5% on a typical sale, with breaks on high-value collectibles.
  • No listing fees. You can prepare large inventories without upfront cost.

Watch-outs

  • Time-intensive. Preparing, hosting and shipping after shows is a lot of work.
  • Low starting bids carry risk. Starting auctions at very low prices can mean selling below value.
  • Audience building takes time. New sellers may host shows with very few viewers at first.
  • Approval and rules. Seller onboarding, category rules and show standards apply.
  • Giveaway costs. Giveaways bring viewers but you pay shipping on them.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Keep a consistent show schedule so followers know when to tune in.
  2. Prepare and photograph your inventory before going live, and pre-list items so shows run smoothly.
  3. Mix low-start auctions with fixed-price items to balance excitement and margin.
  4. Use giveaways strategically, such as when you hit a viewer milestone, rather than constantly.
  5. Talk about each item's condition, history and value on camera; buyers want context.
  6. Ship within your promised time with secure packaging, especially for cards and fragile collectibles.
  7. Watch successful shows in your category to learn pacing, lighting and how hosts keep chat engaged.

Buyer protection in brief

Orders placed through Whatnot are covered by its buyer protection if items do not arrive, arrive damaged, or are not as described. Buyers can request help through the app, and Whatnot reviews the case. Sellers should keep shows clearly recorded, describe condition accurately on camera and in listings, and ship with tracking. Transactions arranged off the app are not protected.

Live selling creates a natural record: what you say on camera about condition and grading is part of what the buyer relied on. Be precise about flaws, centering, or wear, and avoid hype that overstates an item. For cards and small collectibles, use sleeves, top loaders and rigid mailers, and photograph high-value orders before sealing them. Those habits reduce damage claims and make any dispute faster to resolve.
