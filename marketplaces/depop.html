Depop: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

A style-led resale app where vintage, streetwear and one-of-a-kind pieces find a younger crowd.

At a glance

  • Fashion resale with a strong focus on vintage, streetwear and personal style.
  • US sellers pay no selling fee on listings created since July 15, 2024.
  • A payment processing fee of 3.3% + $0.45 applies to each sale.
  • Buyers pay a separate marketplace fee at checkout.
  • Optional Boosted Listings cost 12% only when a boosted item sells.

Who it's best for

Depop rewards sellers with a clear aesthetic. Vintage dealers, thrift flippers, streetwear resellers and small brands that make their own clothing do well, especially when their shop looks curated. The audience skews younger and style-driven, so trend-led items, graphic tees, denim and accessories tend to move faster than plain basics.

It is less suited to electronics, home goods or very high-end luxury items, and sellers who prefer formal business tools may find it a little lightweight.

Because there is no US selling fee, Depop is also a sensible second channel for fashion resellers who already list elsewhere. The cost of a sale here is mostly payment processing, so cross-listing your best vintage and streetwear pieces adds reach without adding much expense. The trade-off is time: Depop buyers respond to shops that look curated and active, so a few neglected listings tend to perform poorly compared with a well-maintained storefront that adds new stock regularly.

What it costs to sell

For US-based sellers, Depop removed its selling fee in 2024, so the platform itself takes nothing from the item price on new listings. The cost you do pay is payment processing through Depop Payments: 3.3% of the transaction plus $0.45. That percentage applies to the item price, the shipping charge and any tax the buyer pays.

Buyers pay their own marketplace fee at checkout, which does not reduce your earnings. If you choose to use Boosted Listings, Depop charges 12% on boosted sales, counted only when a buyer views your boosted listing and buys it within 28 days. When a sale is refunded, Depop reverses the fees.

Worked example ($50 item, free shipping, no tax, not boosted):

  • Payment processing: (3.3% × $50) + $0.45 = $1.65 + $0.45 = $2.10
  • You receive: $50.00 − $2.10 = $47.90, before your shipping cost.

If the sale came from a Boost: add 12% × $50 = $6.00, so you would receive $41.90.

Fees verified October 2026 on Depop's official seller fees and charges help page and its Boosted Listings policy.

Strengths

  • Low seller costs. With no selling fee, only processing comes out of your sale.
  • Style-focused audience. Buyers browse for looks, not just brands, which helps unbranded vintage.
  • Shop as storefront. A cohesive grid of photos can build a following.
  • Simple app. Listing and messaging are fast and mobile-native.
  • Pay-for-results promotion. Boosting is only charged when it leads to a sale.

Watch-outs

  • Buyer fees can affect conversion. The checkout fee raises the price buyers see at the end.
  • Narrow category fit. Non-fashion items get far less attention.
  • Boost costs add up. At 12%, frequent boosting can erase the no-fee advantage.
  • Haggling and messages. Buyers often negotiate in chat and may ask for off-platform deals, which you should refuse.
  • Fees can change. Depop's structure has shifted more than once; recheck periodically.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Photograph items on a model or styled flat lay against a consistent background so your shop grid looks curated.
  2. Use descriptive style words buyers search for, such as era, fit and aesthetic, alongside brand and size.
  3. Add accurate measurements; they reduce returns and questions.
  4. Refresh listings and drop prices modestly on older stock to bring them back into view.
  5. Use Boost selectively on higher-margin items, since the 12% fee is easier to absorb there.
  6. Reply to messages quickly and keep negotiation on-platform to stay covered.
  7. Offer bundle discounts so buyers combine items into one shipment.

Buyer protection in brief

Purchases paid through Depop Payments are covered by Depop's buyer protection if an item does not arrive or is significantly not as described. Buyers can raise an issue with the seller and escalate to Depop if it is not resolved. Deals arranged outside the app, or paid by other means, are not protected. Sellers should ship with tracking, describe flaws clearly and keep all conversations in the app.

When a buyer opens a case, respond quickly and calmly, share your photos of the item before it shipped, and offer a return or partial refund if the complaint is reasonable. Resolving issues inside the app keeps your record clean and avoids escalation. Remember that a refunded sale also has its fees reversed, so a fair refund costs you less than it might seem. Packing photos are especially useful for vintage pieces, where small marks are often the source of disputes about condition.
