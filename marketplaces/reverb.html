Reverb: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

A specialist destination for buying and selling musical instruments, pro audio and music gear.

At a glance

  • Built specifically for guitars, keyboards, drums, pedals, amps, studio gear and other music equipment.
  • No listing fees; you pay only when an item sells.
  • A 5% selling fee on the item price plus shipping, with a $0.50 minimum and $500 maximum.
  • Payment processing through Reverb Payments costs 3.19% + $0.49 in the US.
  • Price guide data helps sellers and buyers judge fair market value for common gear.

Who it's best for

Reverb is the natural home for anyone selling music gear, from a musician parting with a single pedal to vintage guitar dealers and full-line music stores. Buyers on the platform know the products, which means detailed specs and honest condition notes get rewarded, and niche or vintage items can find the right audience. The fee cap on very expensive items makes it particularly attractive for high-value instruments.

If you sell mostly non-musical goods, Reverb's audience is too specialized to be useful. Sellers of inexpensive accessories such as strings or cables can still use it, but the $0.50 minimum fee and fixed processing charge take a larger bite out of low-priced sales.

What it costs to sell

Reverb's fees come in two parts. The selling fee is 5% of the total transaction value, which includes the item price and shipping but not sales tax. The minimum selling fee is $0.50 and the maximum is $500 for sales up to $10,000. Listing is free and there is no monthly membership.

The second part is payment processing through Reverb Payments. For US sellers, it is 3.19% plus $0.49 per transaction. Reverb Preferred Sellers, who meet certain performance standards, pay a lower 2.99% plus $0.49. Other possible charges include currency conversion for international sales and optional paid bumps for extra visibility.

Worked example ($50 item, free shipping, US seller):

  • Selling fee: 5% × $50 = $2.50
  • Payment processing: (3.19% × $50) + $0.49 = $1.595 + $0.49 = $2.085
  • Total fees: $2.50 + $2.085 = $4.585, or about $4.59
  • You receive: about $45.41, before your shipping cost.

If you charge the buyer $20 for shipping, both fees apply to $70 instead: $3.50 selling plus $2.72 processing, for about $6.22 in total. That is why many Reverb sellers price shipping close to their real cost rather than padding it.

Fees verified October 2026 on Reverb's official billing policy. Note that Reverb's marketing selling fees page displayed an older processing rate (2.7% + $0.25) at the time of checking; the billing policy is the governing document.

Strengths

  • Targeted buyers. Shoppers are musicians and collectors actively looking for gear.
  • Reasonable fees. Under 10% in total for typical sales, with a cap on high-value items.
  • Price guidance. Sold-price data helps you set realistic prices.
  • Good for vintage and boutique gear. Niche items find knowledgeable buyers.
  • Shop tools. Dealers get inventory management and integrations.

Watch-outs

  • Shipping large items. Amps, drum kits and keyboards are expensive and fragile to ship; damage claims are a real risk.
  • Offer negotiation. Buyers commonly send offers below asking.
  • Narrow audience. Great for gear, irrelevant for anything else.
  • Fees on shipping. The 5% applies to shipping charges as well as the item.
  • Competition from dealers. Popular new items are often listed by stores at sharp prices.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Use the exact make, model, year and finish in your title, and select the matching product in Reverb's catalog where possible.
  2. Photograph serial numbers, electronics, frets, knobs and any wear; Reverb buyers scrutinize details.
  3. Check the price guide for recent sold prices before setting your asking price.
  4. Turn on offers and set an auto-decline floor so you only see serious bids.
  5. Pack instruments in proper cases or double boxes and buy shipping insurance for valuable items.
  6. Offer local pickup for heavy gear to reach nearby buyers and avoid freight costs.
  7. Work toward Preferred Seller status by shipping fast and maintaining good ratings, which lowers your processing fee.

Buyer protection in brief

Purchases made through Reverb's checkout are covered by Reverb Protection, which helps buyers if an item does not arrive or is not as described. Buyers contact the seller first and can escalate to Reverb if the issue is not resolved. Sellers are protected when they ship with tracking to the address on the order and describe items accurately. Deals made outside Reverb's checkout are not covered.

For instruments, document condition thoroughly before shipping: photos of the neck, headstock, electronics and case, plus a note of any setup work. Fragile gear is often damaged in transit, so packing well and insuring the shipment protects both you and the buyer. If a claim arises, those photos and the carrier's insurance are usually what decide the outcome.
