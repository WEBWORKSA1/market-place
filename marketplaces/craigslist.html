Craigslist: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

The no-frills classifieds board where most for-sale posts are still free and deals happen face to face.

At a glance

  • Text-heavy local classifieds organized by city and category.
  • No commission: payment happens directly between buyer and seller.
  • Most by-owner for-sale posts are free.
  • In the US, posts for cars and trucks, RVs and motorcycles by owner cost $5, as do for-sale posts by dealers.
  • Contact is through anonymized email relay, phone or text, depending on what you choose to share.

Who it's best for

Craigslist suits sellers who want a simple, free listing to reach local buyers, especially for bulky goods such as furniture, appliances, building materials, garden equipment and used vehicles. It also attracts buyers who prefer browsing outside social networks. People who are comfortable vetting strangers by email or phone and arranging a safe meetup will find it a straightforward channel.

It is not a good choice if you need shipping, payment processing, or any kind of platform-backed protection.

Craigslist is also worth considering for free and "curb alert" items you simply want gone, for building leftovers like tile and lumber, and for large items in rural areas where app-based buyers are thin. Its category structure includes sections such as farm and garden, materials and heavy equipment, which can reach buyers who are not active on newer apps. Sellers posting the same item across several local sites often keep Craigslist in the mix because it costs nothing for typical goods.

What it costs to sell

Craigslist does not charge a commission on sales, and most postings are free. In its posting fees list, the exceptions for for-sale items are a $5 fee for all by-dealer for-sale categories in the US, cars and trucks by dealer, and US posts for cars and trucks, RVs and motorcycles by owner. Other categories, such as jobs and some housing in certain cities, have their own fees, but those are not part of selling goods.

There are no payment or processing fees because Craigslist does not handle money at all.

Worked example ($50 item, by owner, sold in person):

  • Craigslist fee: $0.00
  • You receive: $50.00.

Used car by owner in the US: a single $5 posting fee, regardless of sale price.

Fees verified October 2026 on Craigslist's official posting fees page.

Strengths

  • Free for most items. No commission and no listing fee for typical by-owner posts.
  • Simple. Posting takes minutes and does not require a social media profile.
  • Local buyers. Good for items people want to see in person.
  • Privacy options. Email relay lets you avoid sharing your personal address.
  • Vehicles at low cost. A $5 post is far cheaper than many dedicated car sites.

Watch-outs

  • Scams are common. Fake checks, overpayment requests, shipping-only buyers and code-verification schemes target sellers.
  • No protection. Craigslist is not involved in the transaction and does not mediate disputes.
  • Dated interface. Fewer features than modern apps; photos and search are basic.
  • Flagging. Posts can be removed if other users flag them, even for minor rule issues.
  • Safety. Meeting strangers requires care, especially for high-value items.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Post in the most specific category and city area, so local buyers browsing that section see you.
  2. Include several clear photos and key details like brand, model, dimensions and condition to filter out casual inquiries.
  3. Put the price in the post and a short line about how you want to be contacted and paid.
  4. Renew or repost according to the site's rules to stay near the top of the listings.
  5. Meet in a public place for small items, and have someone with you when a buyer comes to your home for large ones.
  6. Accept cash or a payment you can verify instantly; never accept checks for more than the price or ship to a buyer you have not met.
  7. Ignore anyone who asks you to share a verification code or move to a "secure" payment link.

Buyer protection in brief

Craigslist offers no buyer or seller protection. It does not process payments, guarantee items or settle disputes. Both sides depend on in-person inspection, careful communication and safe meeting habits. If you encounter a scam, you can flag the post, but recovering money is generally not possible through Craigslist. Treat every deal as final once money and goods change hands.

A few habits go a long way. Use the anonymized email relay until you are comfortable with a buyer, and share a phone number only when you are ready to arrange pickup. Count cash on the spot, or confirm an instant payment in your own banking app rather than trusting a screenshot. Keep a short note of who bought what and when, and for vehicles, complete a written bill of sale and follow your state's title transfer rules so liability passes to the new owner.
