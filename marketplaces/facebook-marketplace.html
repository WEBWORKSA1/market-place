Facebook Marketplace: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

A buy-and-sell feed inside Facebook that turns your local community into ready buyers.

At a glance

  • Listings live inside Facebook, so a huge existing audience can see them without installing another app.
  • Local pickup sales with cash or your own payment app carry no Facebook selling fee.
  • Shipped orders paid through Facebook's checkout incur a selling fee of 10%, or $0.80 on small orders.
  • Messenger handles buyer conversations, which is fast but invites lots of low-effort inquiries.
  • Protection is limited to eligible orders that go through Facebook's checkout.

Who it's best for

Facebook Marketplace shines for items that are expensive or awkward to ship: furniture, appliances, exercise equipment, baby gear, tools and outdoor items. It also works well for quick declutters, because listing takes a minute from a phone and buyers nearby can pick up the same day. Sellers who already have a Facebook account and are comfortable messaging strangers will find it the fastest way to turn unused things into cash.

For shipped sales, it is a reasonable option for clothing, collectibles and small electronics, but buyers there tend to be less committed than on dedicated resale apps.

What it costs to sell

If you sell locally and the buyer pays you in person, Facebook does not charge you anything. Fees only apply when you sell with shipping and the buyer pays through Facebook's checkout. In that case, the selling fee is 10% of the order total, which includes the shipping charge the buyer pays (and, according to third-party guides, any tax). For small shipments of $8 or less, a flat $0.80 applies instead.

Shipping labels and how they are purchased can change, so check the shipping options in the app before you list. Boosting a listing with ads is optional and billed separately.

Worked example ($50 item, shipped, buyer pays no separate shipping):

  • Selling fee: 10% × $50 = $5.00
  • You receive: $50.00 − $5.00 = $45.00, before the cost of shipping.

Same item, local pickup with cash: fee $0.00, you keep $50.00.

Verification note: Facebook's official fees for sales page could not be read by our automated check. The 10% and $0.80 figures were confirmed against independent seller guides updated in September 2026, verified October 2026. Confirm in the app before relying on them for large sales.

Strengths

  • Huge built-in audience. Buyers are already scrolling Facebook, so listings get seen quickly.
  • Zero cost locally. No commission on in-person sales makes it hard to beat for bulky goods.
  • Speed. Many items sell within a day or two, especially when priced fairly.
  • Profiles add context. Buyers and sellers can see some public profile information, which can help assess who you are dealing with.
  • Simple listing flow. Photos, a price and a short description are enough.

Watch-outs

  • Scams and no-shows. Fake payment screenshots, overpayment tricks and requests to move to other apps are common; ghosting is frequent.
  • Low-effort messages. Expect many "is this available?" messages that go nowhere.
  • Limited protection for local deals. Cash or peer-to-peer payments are not covered by Facebook's purchase protection.
  • Policy enforcement. Listings can be removed by automated systems for unclear reasons, and appeals can be slow.
  • Fees on shipping. The 10% fee applies to the shipping charge as well as the item.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Use your first photo to show the whole item in good light; it is the thumbnail buyers scroll past in the feed.
  2. Put the brand, item type and key size or measurement in the title, since buyers search by keyword and filter by distance.
  3. Price slightly above your minimum to leave room for negotiation, which buyers on this platform expect.
  4. Answer quickly and suggest a specific pickup time and public place; delays let buyers drift to other listings.
  5. For local sales, accept cash or instant payment in person and never ship before you are paid through a method you trust.
  6. Renew or lightly edit older listings so they reappear for nearby buyers.
  7. Cross-post larger listings to relevant local buy-and-sell groups to reach additional buyers.

Buyer protection in brief

Facebook's Purchase Protection applies to eligible orders that are paid and shipped through its checkout. Buyers can request a refund if an item does not arrive, arrives damaged, or is significantly different from the listing. Local pickups paid in cash or through outside payment apps are not covered, so both sides rely on meeting safely and inspecting items before paying. Sellers shipping through checkout should use tracking and keep photos of what they packed.

For in-person sales, the safest approach is simple: meet in a busy public place or a police station exchange zone, bring someone with you for larger items, and complete the payment before the item leaves your hands. Be wary of buyers who ask for a verification code, insist on paying by an unfamiliar method, or offer to send a courier. Those are common signs of a scam rather than a genuine sale.
