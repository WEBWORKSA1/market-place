eBay: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

The veteran auction-and-fixed-price site where almost anything, new or used, can find a buyer.

At a glance

  • Sell by auction or fixed price ("Buy It Now"), with optional Best Offer.
  • Final value fee of 13.6% in most categories, plus a small per-order fee.
  • 250 free listings per month for most sellers before insertion fees apply.
  • Strong for used goods, parts, collectibles and hard-to-find items.
  • Built-in shipping labels, international shipping programs and promoted listings.

Who it's best for

eBay works well for resellers who source a wide variety of items, from vintage clothing and electronics to auto parts and trading cards. Its search engine is tuned for specific queries such as model numbers, so buyers who know exactly what they want often end up here. Collectors value the auction format for pricing rare items, and casual sellers can list a few things a month without paying anything up front.

It is less ideal if you want a fully hands-off experience or you only sell bulky local items, where a classifieds app avoids shipping altogether.

What it costs to sell

The main charge is the final value fee, taken when an item sells. For most categories it is 13.6% of the total sale amount up to $7,500, and 2.35% on any portion above that. "Total sale amount" means more than the item price: eBay includes shipping charged to the buyer, handling and sales tax in the calculation. On top of that there is a per-order fee of $0.30 for orders of $10 or less and $0.40 for orders above $10.

Some categories have different rates, so check the category before you list high-value items. Insertion fees are usually not an issue for smaller sellers: you get 250 zero-insertion-fee listings each month, and after that each new listing costs $0.35. Optional extras like promoted listings, listing upgrades and store subscriptions are separate.

Worked example ($50 item, free shipping, most categories):

  • Final value fee: 13.6% × $50 = $6.80
  • Per-order fee (order over $10): $0.40
  • Total fees: $6.80 + $0.40 = $7.20
  • You receive: $50.00 − $7.20 = $42.80, before shipping costs and any sales tax effect.

Because eBay applies the percentage to tax as well, your real fee on a taxed order will be slightly higher than this example shows.

Fees verified October 2026 on eBay's official selling fees page.

Strengths

  • Range of buyers. People come to eBay for both everyday and obscure items, which helps with things other sites cannot move.
  • Flexible formats. Auctions help discover a price; fixed price with Best Offer suits steady sellers.
  • Low upfront cost. The monthly free listing allowance means you can test the platform without spending.
  • Mature tools. Bulk listing, discounted shipping labels, and detailed sales reports are built in.
  • Global reach. International programs let you sell abroad without handling customs yourself.

Watch-outs

  • Fees on shipping and tax. The percentage applies to the whole order total, not just your item price.
  • Promoted listing pressure. Many sellers feel they need to pay for ad placement to stay visible in competitive categories.
  • Buyer-friendly disputes. Returns and "not as described" claims tend to be decided in the buyer's favor if evidence is unclear.
  • Performance standards. Late shipping and defects affect your seller level and visibility.
  • Category differences. Some categories carry different final value rates, so always confirm before pricing.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Put the exact model, brand, size and condition in the first part of your title; eBay buyers search with very specific terms.
  2. Fill in every item specific (the structured attributes), since buyers filter by them.
  3. Research completed and sold listings before pricing, rather than relying on active asking prices.
  4. Offer free shipping by building the cost into the price, which simplifies comparisons for buyers.
  5. Enable Best Offer with auto-accept and auto-decline thresholds so you negotiate without constant messages.
  6. Ship within your stated handling time and upload tracking; this protects you in disputes and helps your seller standing.
  7. Use promoted listings selectively on items in crowded categories, starting at a low ad rate and adjusting based on results.

Buyer protection in brief

eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases paid through its checkout when an item does not arrive, arrives damaged, or does not match the listing. Buyers open a request, the seller has a chance to resolve it, and eBay can step in if they cannot agree. Sellers have some protections too, such as removal of negative feedback in certain cases and safeguards against abusive buyers, but the system leans toward keeping buyers confident. Clear photos, honest condition notes and tracked shipping are the best ways to protect yourself. For higher-value orders, signature confirmation adds another layer of evidence that the buyer received the package.
