Walmart Marketplace: fees, pros, cons & selling tips

Third-party selling on Walmart.com for established sellers who want big-box reach without a subscription.

At a glance

  • Third-party sellers list alongside Walmart's own inventory on Walmart.com.
  • No setup or monthly subscription fees; you pay a referral fee only when something sells.
  • The default referral fee is 15%, with tiered or lower rates in some categories.
  • Sellers must apply and be approved, and the program favors businesses with a track record.
  • Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) offers optional warehousing and shipping for an extra fee.

Who it's best for

Walmart Marketplace fits sellers who already run a real e-commerce business: brand owners, distributors and experienced resellers with a US business presence, reliable stock and the ability to ship quickly. It is often used as a second major channel by sellers already active on Amazon, because many listing and fulfillment habits carry over.

It is not designed for casual sellers, one-off used items, or people testing an idea. Approval is required, and sellers are measured on shipping speed, cancellation rates and customer service.

The lack of a monthly fee makes Walmart appealing for sellers with modest volume who still meet the bar for approval. You can keep a smaller catalog live without paying a subscription each month, as long as you keep inventory accurate and orders moving on time.

What it costs to sell

There is no monthly fee and no setup fee. Walmart charges a referral fee, a percentage of the total sales price, when an order completes. That total includes the item price plus any shipping, handling and gift-wrap charges.

The default rate for categories not otherwise listed ("Everything Else") is 15%. Several categories are tiered by price. For example, apparel is 5% up to $15, 10% between $15 and $20, and 15% above $20; beauty and baby items are 8% at $10 or less and 15% above; electronics accessories are 15% on the first $100 and 8% beyond that; and jewelry is 20% on the first $250 and 5% above. Fulfillment through WFS, advertising and returns handling are separate costs.

Worked example ($50 item, general category, free shipping):

  • Referral fee: 15% × $50 = $7.50
  • You receive: $50.00 − $7.50 = $42.50, before your product, shipping and advertising costs.

If you charge the buyer $5 shipping, the fee applies to $55, making it $8.25.

Fees verified October 2026 on Walmart's official referral fees page.

Strengths

  • No subscription. You pay only when you sell, which lowers fixed costs compared with paid seller plans.
  • Less crowded than some rivals. Because entry is gated, some categories have fewer competing third-party sellers.
  • Strong brand trust. Shoppers know the Walmart name, which helps conversion for unfamiliar sellers.
  • Fulfillment option. WFS can make items eligible for fast-shipping badges.
  • Tiered rates help low-priced goods. Inexpensive apparel and beauty items pay reduced percentages.

Watch-outs

  • Approval hurdle. Applications require business details and selling history, and not everyone is accepted.
  • Strict performance standards. On-time shipping, valid tracking and low cancellation rates are monitored.
  • Price parity expectations. Listings priced well above other sites may lose visibility.
  • Listing setup. Item setup relies on product identifiers and structured data, which takes some learning.
  • Fees on shipping. The referral fee applies to shipping charges too.

Tips to sell more here

  1. Prepare your application with your business tax details, a history of e-commerce sales and a clear product catalog to improve your chances of approval.
  2. Start with products that already sell well for you elsewhere, so you are not testing demand and a new channel at the same time.
  3. Match or beat your prices on other sites; uncompetitive pricing can reduce how often your offer is shown.
  4. Fill every attribute and use high-resolution images; complete listings perform better in Walmart's search.
  5. Offer two-day or faster shipping where possible, either yourself or through WFS, to earn fast-delivery badges.
  6. Keep inventory counts synced to avoid cancellations, which hurt your performance score.
  7. Test Walmart's sponsored product ads on your best sellers with a small daily budget before scaling.

Buyer protection in brief

Orders from Marketplace sellers are covered by Walmart's customer care and return policies, so buyers can seek help if an item is late, damaged or not as described. Sellers are expected to follow Walmart's return policy and respond quickly to customer issues; Walmart can step in and issue refunds. Accurate listings, tracked shipping and fast responses keep disputes, and their costs, to a minimum.

Returns deserve special planning. Decide in advance how you will handle items that come back, whether you will restock, refurbish or liquidate them, and factor a realistic return rate into your pricing for categories like apparel and electronics. Using valid tracking on every order also protects your performance metrics if a buyer claims a package never arrived.
